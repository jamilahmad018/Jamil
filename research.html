Impact of Political Stability on Renewable Energy Consumption

Evidence from ASEAN Countries — Master's Thesis at Universitas Islam Internasional Indonesia (UIII), investigating how political governance influences Southeast Asia's renewable energy transition.

9 ASEAN Countries
2000–2023 Study Period
RE GLS Estimation Method
-3.799 PS Coefficient

❓ Research Question

Does political stability increase or decrease the share of renewable energy in a country's total final energy consumption? The thesis tests this across nine ASEAN economies (ASEAN-9) over the period 2000–2023, evaluating governance performance alongside key macroeconomic control factors.

📚 Theoretical Grounding

The study is theoretically grounded in three main pillars to understand how institutional quality interacts with national energy choices:

  • Institutional Theory: Explaining governance, regulatory incentives, and structural stability.
  • Sustainable Development Theory: Balancing economic acceleration with long-term ecological balance.
  • Energy Transition Theory: Analyzing structural shifts from fossil fuels to clean renewable alternatives.

🔬 Methodology & Dataset

Dependent Variable: Renewable Energy Consumption (REC) — measured as renewable energy's percentage share of total final energy consumption.

Focal Independent Variable: Political Stability (PS) — drawn from the World Bank's World Governance Indicators (WGI).

Control Variables: Log-transformed GDP per capita (with a quadratic term to capture non-linear relationships), Foreign Direct Investment (FDI), Trade Openness, and Inflation rate.

Sample & Estimation: Balanced panel data for nine ASEAN countries (2000–2023). Estimated using Random Effects GLS regression with cluster-robust standard errors in Stata. The Hausman test (χ² = 2.12, p = 0.833) statistically supported the selection of Random Effects over Fixed Effects.

📊 Key Findings & Empirical Results

Political stability showed a statistically significant negative relationship with renewable energy consumption (β = −3.799). Control variables including FDI, trade openness, and inflation were not statistically significant in the primary model specification.

Additionally, the quadratic GDP-per-capita variable revealed a U-shaped relationship between economic income and renewable energy consumption, with an estimated turning point around USD 39,500 per capita.

💡 Policy & Institutional Implications

A negative relationship between political stability and renewable energy adoption appears counter-intuitive at first. However, institutional theory suggests that highly stable governments in developing markets may prioritize entrenched fossil-fuel infrastructure, state subsidies, and incumbent power grids over rapid transition investments in the short term. Policy frameworks must align long-term stability with targeted green energy incentives.
